Cost-Per-Lead Math

Starting inputs are conservative planning estimates for cold Meta traffic to a free B2B offer aimed at e-commerce owners in the US, CA, UK and AU: about $200 per lead and $500 per booked call. The landing page conversion is set low because the form asks for store URL and revenue, which filters out tyre-kickers, and 80% qualify because every ad calls out "$30K+ a month". Client value assumes a $4K/month retainer for 6 months. Swap in Arcady's real numbers after the first week of spend. Why a $200 lead still works: about 14 booked calls and 2 new clients from the first month's ~$7K, worth roughly 7x the ad spend over six months. The number to watch is cost per booked call, not cost per lead.

Biggest Bottleneck

And how the system fixes it
The problem

Lead → booked call

Free offers attract curious people. Getting a cheap lead is easy; getting a qualified store owner onto a call is where free-audit funnels break. If 100 people request an audit and only 10 book, the real cost per call is 10x the cost per lead.

  • Qualify before the call. Revenue band in the form; only $30K+/mo stores are offered a call straight away.
  • Book while intent is highest. Qualified applicants go straight to the calendar, and email 1 arrives instantly with the booking link.
  • Give value first. The audit is worth having on its own, so the call is a walkthrough of findings, not a sales pitch.
  • Chase the no-shows. Emails at 24h and 48h, then call reminders by email and SMS.
  • Optimise for the right event. Track QualifiedLead and Schedule, and switch Meta's optimisation to booked calls once there's enough volume.
